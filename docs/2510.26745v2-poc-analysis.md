**Purpose.** (1) Validate the markdown conversion (`docs/internal/2510.26745v2.md` + `docs/internal/2510.26745v2_images/`, untracked) against the source PDF; (2) give an impartial assessment of what a greenfield Rust POC in this repository can credibly demonstrate, at what cost, with what risks. ### 1.3 Errata in the paper itself (PDF-faithful; a reimplementation must resolve each)

1. **Sign flip in Prop. 7's induction** (md 1185, p71): text writes `C = D⁻¹A + (D⁻¹A)ᵀ **+** (P + Pᵀ)`, contradicting Lemma 6's Eq. (3) `−(P + Pᵀ)`. The minus is correct: Fact 1 (C(0) ≈ −L) only holds with the minus.
2. **Bit-complexity swap** (md 1072, p66): Refutation 3d's proof says associative memory costs "|V| log |E|"; Proposition 1 (md 1052) says `|E| log |V|`. Prop. 1 is the derived form. For path-star/cycle, |E| ≈ |V|, so the conclusion is unaffected.
3. **Eq. (11) denominator** (md 1248, p73): `exp(u, v')` — should be `exp(u · v')`.
4. **Empty equation (17)** (md 1272, p73): a numbered equation with no content, in the PDF as well; the derivation resumes with "By writing the above expressions as a matrix formula…".
5. **Proposition/Lemma cross-naming**: Appendix E/F statements are labeled Proposition 1–5 but cited elsewhere as Lemma 1–5 (e.g., md 1072, 1090; footnote 12 at md 388).
6. **Variable slip in Prop. 3's proof** (md 1084, p67): "For w ∈ V₂, Φ(v) at index i…" mixes w and v.
7. **"Observation 1" citation** (md 1214, p71): "the convergence of the null space … from Observation 1" — context indicates Observation 7.
8. **100 vs ~200 steps**: §3.2 text says geometric memory "requires 100 steps (Fig. 8)" (md 256); Fig. 8's caption says "about a 200 steps or more" (md 302). Treat as order-of-magnitude 10², not a pinned constant.

## 2. What the paper claims — inventory

### 2.1 Setting

- **Graph.** Path-star G_{d,ℓ}: root with d disjoint arms; the i-th arm is (v_root = v₀, v₁, …, v_{ℓ−1}), so ℓ counts nodes including the shared root and the graph has d(ℓ−1)+1 nodes (B.1, md 753). Confirmed by G_{10⁴,6} ≈ 5×10⁴ nodes (Obs. 1, md 172).
- **Data.** (i) Edge-memorization bigrams "u v", forward and reverse directions (D→_edge ∪ D←_edge); (ii) path-finding examples: prefix = (leaf, [PAUSE]×N), target = full root→leaf path, on 75% of leaves; test on held-out leaves, greedy decoding (B.1, md 757–761). Vocabulary = node tokens + 9 special tokens (md 755).
- **Models.** GPT-mid: decoder-only, 12 layers, width 384 (784 for G_{10⁴,10}), 8 heads, sinusoidal positions, pre-norm, GELU, tied embeddings; Mamba of comparable scale (B.2.1, md 769–781). Tiny models (B.2.2): 1-layer TinyGPT (width 16/32), TinySSM (width 8), TinyNN (width 512; a feed-forward net without attention/residuals). AdamW wd 0.01, cosine schedule with linear warmup, batch ∈ {64…1024}, pause tokens ∈ {0,2,4,6,10} (B.3; Table 2, md 805–828).

## 3. Reproducibility assessment

**Fully specified** (validated against PDF): graph construction and node counts; data formats for both example types; GPT-mid and tiny architectures; Table 2 per-graph hyperparameters; optimizer family/weight decay/schedule shape; batch-size and pause-token grids; metrics (exact-match path accuracy, first-token accuracy, per-token accuracy; 1/d chance baseline); the Node2Vec objective (Eq. 1), its dynamics (Lemma 6), and the instrumentation of Fig. 9 (track ‖Vᵀeᵢ‖₂ and ‖Ceᵢ‖₂ against eigenvectors of −L; m=100).

**Partially specified / unspecified** (each is a decision the POC must make and log):

1. Warmup length ("linear warm-up", md 809 — no step count).
2. Node2Vec learning rate, step count, and stopping criterion for Fig. 9 — §B covers the sequence models; Fig. 9's caption gives only m=100.
3. Initialization scale: Assumption 1 requires "sufficiently large magnitude or embedding dimensionality" (md 1157); c is never pinned.
4. Edge:path interleaving ratio within a training batch/epoch (md 154 says interleaved; ratio unstated).
5. "Epoch" size — dataset cardinalities per epoch are not defined for the mixed dataset.
6. TinyNN internals: "feed-forward neural network with the same configuration but without residual connection or attention" (md 787) — hidden width and nonlinearity placement are not spelled out beyond the GPT-mid GELU convention.
7. Tiny irregular graph topology: figure-only, no adjacency list. Tiny path-star "four paths of length 4" (md 878) is ambiguous between 13 nodes (ℓ=4 in the B.1 node-count convention) and 17 (4 edges per arm).
8. Seed/replication counts: §B (md 749–841) does not state how many runs per figure.
9. Early-stopping criterion for the Node2Vec degenerate-vector drift (Remark 5, md 1149): "we may need to early-stop to prevent collapse" — no criterion given.
10. No code-release statement was found: `rg -in "github|code release|open.source|available at"` over the markdown (excluding citation URLs) returns no such statement, and the pages 1–19/20–42 validators reported none.

**Compute envelope.** Tiers 1–2 below involve graphs of ≤ ~20 nodes and embeddings of ≤ 512 dims with full-batch GD — trivially CPU-sized. The paper's large-scale runs (C1) used single-GPU PyTorch, A100-40/80GB (B.5, md 840), vocab ~5×10⁴, up to 50,000 epochs (with useful accuracy by "a couple of thousand", md 807).

## 4. POC design space — tiers

Ordered by (claim value)/(effort); each tier is independently publishable as a POC result.

### Tier 0 — Graph + spectral infrastructure

Adjacency/degree construction for path-star, grid, cycle, irregular, tree-star; the asymmetric random-walk Laplacian L = (I − D⁻¹A) + (I − D⁻¹A)ᵀ (md 1104); symmetric eigendecomposition with deterministic ordering; CSV/serialized run logs. Everything downstream consumes this.

*Verifiable ground truth:* the n-cycle's Laplacian eigenvectors are closed-form Fourier modes, and grid-graph spectra follow from Cartesian-product structure — analytic pins for the eigensolver before any learning code runs.

### Tier 1 — Node2Vec spectral dynamics (tests C7, C8, C12; the paper's mechanistic core)

Implement V ∈ ℝ^{n×m}, the Eq. (1) objective, and full-batch gradient ascent. The gradient has the closed form of Lemma 6 — **no autodiff framework is needed**; correctness is checked by finite differences against Eq. (1), which simultaneously settles paper-erratum 1 (the C sign) empirically. Reproduce Fig. 9's three panels per graph: eigenvector-projection trajectories, ‖Vᵀeᵢ‖₂ stabilizing non-zero exactly for Fiedler-like directions, ‖Ceᵢ‖₂ → 0 for those directions. Then:

- **Weight-untied variant** (C8): factorize with two matrices; confirm the near-zero cosine-similarity structure of Figs. 10/33.
- **Observation 8 stress test** (C12): directly measure eigenvector alignment between P+Pᵀ and VVᵀ over training, across all four graphs plus adversarial ones (highly irregular degree — Assumption 2 is violated by path-star already, root degree d vs leaf degree 1). Any failure here is a *finding*, not a POC failure: the paper assumes Obs. 8 with no proof.
- **Remark 5 probe:** run past convergence and characterize degenerate-vector drift; propose a concrete early-stop criterion (the paper leaves this open).

Effort: small. Risk: low — dense linear algebra at n ≤ 20, m ≤ 100.

### Tier 2 — Associative vs. geometric competition in TinyNN (tests C3, C4, C5; the puzzle's refutations)

One trainable matrix between tied (un)embedding layers (B.2.2, md 799–803). Two regimes:

- **Frozen embeddings** (associative): full-batch GD, lr 0.1; metric = fraction of each vertex's k neighbors inside its top-k next-token probabilities, averaged over vertices (Fig. 7 caption, md 298). Target: maximum within 2 steps.
- **Learnable embeddings** (geometric): lr ∈ {0.001, 0.01, 0.1}; track all-pairs cosine distance and heatmaps vs. adjacency (Figs. 8/22–24). Target: geometry at ~10² steps for the two smaller lrs, no geometry at 0.1.

This tier reproduces the timing asymmetry that anchors Refutation 3c and the edge-only-supervision geometry of Refutation 3a on tiny graphs. It requires a nonlinearity/backprop through one hidden layer — still hand-rollable (two matrix products, one GELU), verified by finite differences. Risk: moderate — item 6 in §3 (TinyNN internals) forces documented guesses; if the 2-step result is sensitive to the exact MLP shape, that sensitivity is itself worth reporting.

### Tier 3 — Tiny sequence models (extends C3/C4 to TinyGPT/TinySSM)

1-layer Transformer at width 16–32 over 2-token edge bigrams, and optionally a minimal SSM. A single-layer causal transformer with tied embeddings is implementable by hand, but at this point an autodiff framework (candle or burn) starts paying for itself; whether their SSM support fits TinySSM's spec should be evaluated at this tier's decision point, not assumed now. Value added over Tier 2: confirms architecture-independence of the geometry claim (Obs. 5.3). Effort: medium.

### Tier 4 — Full-scale in-weights path-star (C1, C2, C9, C10, C11, C13, C14)

12×384 GPT-mid, ~5×10⁴-token vocab, interleaved training, pause tokens, GPU. This is a substantial deep-learning engineering effort in Rust for a result the paper already establishes with mature tooling; the scientific payoff of a Rust re-run is low relative to tiers 1–3, and the underspecified items (warmup, interleave ratio, epoch size) make faithful replication a hyperparameter search. Recommended treatment: out of POC scope; revisit only if tiers 1–3 confirm and a large-scale question (e.g., Open Question 2's graph-complexity sweep) motivates it.

## 5. Rust implementation considerations

- **Linear algebra.** POC-scale needs: dense matmul, row-softmax, symmetric eigendecomposition (both C and −L are symmetric by construction — each is X + Xᵀ). Options: `nalgebra` (pure Rust, `SymmetricEigen`, ergonomic at this scale), `faer` (pure Rust, faster at larger n, less ergonomic), `ndarray` + `ndarray-linalg` (LAPACK system dependency). At n ≤ 20 any of them is instant; the choice is ergonomics vs. avoiding a native dependency, not performance.
- **No autodiff for tiers 0–2.** Lemma 6 gives the Node2Vec gradient in closed form; TinyNN backprop is two layers. Finite-difference checks make hand gradients as trustworthy as a framework, and keep the dependency tree flat.
- **Precision.** f64 throughout tiers 0–2: eigenvalue gaps near zero (the degenerate top eigenvector sits "slightly above zero", Remark 5, md 1149) are exactly where f32 noise would confound the ‖Ceᵢ‖₂ → 0 measurement.
- **Determinism.** Seeded RNG (e.g., `rand` + a counter-based or ChaCha generator) with the seed logged per run; full-batch GD makes runs bit-reproducible, which turns every figure into a regression test.
- **Runtime.** Tiers 0–2 are synchronous CPU numerics; the existing `tokio` dependency in `Cargo.toml` is not needed by them. Keep or drop based on whether later tiers or tooling (e.g., a sweep runner) want it.
- **Output.** CSV per-step logs (eigenprojections, losses, metrics) + external plotting, or `plotters` for in-repo figure generation. CSV-first keeps the compute core free of rendering deps.

## 6. Verification plan (falsifiable pins)

1. **Eigensolver pin:** n-cycle Fiedler-like eigenvectors vs. closed-form Fourier modes; tolerance-checked.
2. **Gradient pin:** Lemma 6's ηCV vs. central finite differences of Eq. (1) at random V, per graph. This also adjudicates paper-erratum 1: with the wrong sign, C(0) ≈ +L and the finite-difference check fails loudly.
3. **Dynamics pins (Fig. 9 signatures):** after convergence on each tiny graph — (a) ‖Vᵀeᵢ‖₂ above a threshold exactly for the Fiedler-like index set, below for the rest; (b) ‖Ceᵢ‖₂ below tolerance for that same set. Falsify by perturbing: an untied run must break (a)'s pattern (C8).
4. **Timing pin (Tier 2):** associative metric saturates at step ≤ 2 with lr 0.1; geometric cosine-structure emerges within an order of magnitude of 10² steps at lr 0.001. The paper's own 100-vs-200 discrepancy (erratum 8) means the pin asserts the *ratio* (≥ ~50×), not an exact step count.
5. **Observation 8 pin:** eigenvector alignment (e.g., subspace principal angles between top-k of P+Pᵀ and VVᵀ) stays above threshold during training on the paper's four graphs — and the same measurement is *reported, not asserted,* on adversarial-degree graphs.

Each pin should be falsified once (revert/perturb, watch it fail, restore) before being trusted, and the failure message should carry the measured value.

## 7. Risks

1. **Observation 8 is an assumption.** The paper's own proof chain (Props. 7–11) leans on it "without even an intuitive proof" (md 1165). If it degrades on some graphs, the Tier 1 dynamics may not match Fig. 9 there. Mitigation: measure it explicitly (pin 5); a violation is a reportable result.
2. **Assumption 2 (uniform degree) is false on path-star** (root degree d, internal 2, leaf 1). The paper says the propositions "hold in practice without our simplifying assumptions (at least in the graphs we study)" (md 1153) — the POC tests exactly this.
3. **Underspecified knobs** (§3 items 1–9) mean quantitative deviations from the paper's figures are expected; the POC should treat qualitative signatures (which eigenvectors survive, the timing ratio, tied-vs-untied contrast) as the replication targets, and log every guessed knob.
4. **Degenerate-vector drift** (Remark 5) can collapse late-training embeddings; without the paper's (unstated) stopping rule, convergence detection needs a home-grown criterion — e.g., stop when the tracked ‖Ceᵢ‖₂ set plateaus.
5. **Tier ≥ 3 tooling** (Rust autodiff/SSM support) is unassessed here by design; it is a decision-point item, not a prerequisite.

## 8. Recommendation

Scope the POC to **Tiers 0–2**: a CPU-only, framework-free crate that (a) reproduces the Node2Vec spectral-bias dynamics of §4/Appendix F with the Fig. 9 instrumentation, (b) reproduces the associative-vs-geometric timing asymmetry and edge-only geometry of §3 on the four tiny graphs, and (c) stress-tests Observation 8 — the one place the POC can *add* evidence beyond replication, aimed squarely at the paper's Open Questions 1–2. Decide on Tier 3 only after Tier 1–2 signatures land. Tier 4 is explicitly out of scope for a POC.

### Decisions (owner, 2026-08-29)

1. **Linear algebra: nalgebra.**
2. **Markdown defects: fixed in place** (see §1.2 status note).
3. **Project structure: mirror `sustia-llc/surrealdb-live-message`** — library-first async lifecycle. Concretely: `src/lib.rs` with `error` (thiserror enum + `Result` alias; bin/tests use anyhow), `logger` (tracing), `settings` (config crate + `config/*.toml`), domain modules (`graph`, `spectral`, …), and `subsystems/` where each long-running component owns an internal `TaskTracker` + `CancellationToken`, exposes `cancellation_token()`, spawns jobs on `child_token()`s, and provides a cancel → close → drain `shutdown()`; no `SubsystemHandle` or signal handling inside the library; a thin daemon `main.rs` wires ctrl-c to the token.
